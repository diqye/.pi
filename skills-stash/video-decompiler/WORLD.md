# The world

**What this page rests on.** Four films that one person accepted, and what he said separated them from the films before them. It is the newest page here and the least tested: the one pair of test films made before and after it came out close (`docs/EVIDENCE.md` at the repository's root). Read its rules as how those four films were made, and say in your report where you left one.

A film is **a world** and **a score**. The score is everything on one clock (SKILL.md's steps build it). The world is
what this page is about, and it is what separates a film people call clean from one they remember: the subject it is
found in, the picture board behind it, the meaning in each change of scene, and the moments nobody asked for.

A clean film with no world reads as a competent freelancer's. The films this skill is built from each had one. Their
boards hold it in the one idea, the cast and the rules, written before any code; the sections headed "The world" were
written out afterwards, for the board's form.

## Find it

1. **Find the world in the subject, cross it with one far thing, and say it in a line.** It has to be true of how the
   subject works. An invitation is one card, and museum paintings are its clothes; every yes is a small firework. A
   test result is a dot, and a proofreader's marks hold the flaky ones. A typed word comes apart into the weather it
   names. A library card opens onto shelf after shelf of old decorated paper.
2. **The name, the mark and the claim are one idea.** "Plus One": the mark is "+1", and a "+1" is what a guest sends
   back. "Stet": the proofreader's word for "let it stand". For a real product, find what in its own name and mark
   the film can make mean something.
3. **Name what this kind of film usually looks like, and leave its treatment, never its subject.** Be the obvious
   choice for the kind first: show what the viewer came to see (the product, the screen, the number). The point of
   view is added on top.
4. **Write the tension:** "A without A's failure, and B without B's failure" (warm without being nostalgic, exact
   without going cold). Check frames against both failures. Try the safe version of the look, and write down why you
   left it.
5. **Rich, not thin.** Concrete things, people, full colour, texture, layers, things in front of things. Flat and
   thin loses. A plain ground is a wall for something rich, and it alternates with a full one. Never choose a look
   because it is easy to build.

## Lay it out before building

Gather what the world is made of (SKILL.md, step 4) and lay it out as one picture: the real pictures, the few
colours and what each means, the faces of type, the mark, one or two frames of the film held against yours. Look at
it. If that sheet would not make a stranger curious, the film is unlikely to: change the world, not the finish.

## The ground

A ground answers a question, or it is dead. Which place am I in? What just happened (rings spread through it)? What
is this made of (the subject's own pattern or artwork, seen close)? How big is this (the ground turns out to be a
card on a bigger ground)? Depth in a flat film is what contains what: a page in a card on a table.

## Every change of scene means something

Decide every boundary. There are five kinds, and "nothing" is not one:

| kind | what it is |
|---|---|
| becomes | the thing turns into the next thing (a typed line grows into the card) |
| carried | something stays through the change: a shape, a person, the camera's own move |
| matched | a cut with the subject in the same place |
| door | a visible threshold into another ground (through a screen, into a card) |
| change of ground | the whole frame changes colour on the music |

The word can become the thing it names. Put the next thing where the eye already is.

## Ties, and moments of wonder

- **Ties.** Things return and answer each other, across picture, words and sound. End by answering the opening: the
  question asked in the first seconds comes back and is answered by the product.
- **Gifts.** Two or three details nobody asked for: the print chosen as a look becomes the night the rest of the film
  happens in; a name's five letters are the film's five sounds. They cost little, and they are what people mention.

## Moves that carry meaning

Substance comes from choosing things that mean something and belong to a story. These are the moves the strongest
films are built on. A board names the two or three it uses and what each one means in this film; a move used with
nothing behind it is a trick.

| move | what it is | where it is seen | how it fails |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The world is the subject** | everything on screen is the subject's own material, so nothing is decoration | Plus One: the invitation, its looks, its guests | the subject's own material is thin and nothing far was crossed with it |
| **The answered opening** | open on a picture or a question that asks; close on it, changed by what happened, so that it answers | "can I bring someone" returns as "can I bring Theo?"; "It passed." returns as "Passed means passed." | the change is too small to see, or the middle did not cause it |
| **Parallels** | two things made to rhyme, so the viewer joins them: a mess and its remedy made of the same people; a trouble and its fix in the same panel | Plus One's chat and its replies; Stet's checks panel twice | the two things are not alike enough to be recognised |
| **One thing carries through** | a small actor is in every part, and the film is what happens to it | Stet's dot; Plus One's card | a mascot stuck on, with nothing happening to it |
| **Things become things** | each thing turns into the next. Morph only what is truly related: a morph between unrelated things claims a link that is not there | the mess falls into a caret; the typed line grows into the card | the two shapes have nothing to do with each other |
| **The word does what it says** | a word is set the way it means, or arrives as its thing is drawn | "rain." comes apart into rain; a film about depth sets "perspective" in perspective | the picture repeats the sentence as a slide |
| **Two rooms, one door** | a change of world happens at a threshold the viewer sees themselves cross; inside one room, one world's rules hold | the chosen print becomes the night; a guest's phone is entered and left | the ground changes on a plain cut and reads as another film |
| **Point of view** | change whose eyes the film looks through, once, at the moment it matters | Plus One leaves the host for one guest's own phone and her press | the switch happens for variety and tells nothing new |
| **Focus is attention** | what is sharp is what matters now; a pull of focus hands attention from one thing to the next | the far cards in Plus One's row are soft | blur laid on as a look, with nothing to choose between |
| **The turn of scale** | the same thing seen from much nearer or much further is another thing: a ground turns out to be a card on a bigger ground | a brand film that backs out through nine nested worlds | the scale changes and the meaning does not |
| **An illusion that resolves** | a picture that reads one way and is then shown to be another: the flat thing is deep, the pattern is a place, the decoration is the product | a poster that turns to show it is a box in space | a puzzle that resolves into nothing the film claims |
| **A colour is a noun** | one colour, kept for one thing | Stet's vermilion is a failed run and nothing else | the accent is spent early and means three things |
| **Case studies** | believable finished work made with the product, each a small complete thing, shown large | Plus One's five dressed invitations | placeholder content: grey boxes, "Item 1", food with no picture |
| **The proof is the film** | the film is made of, or with, the thing it sells, and says so at the right moment | a design tool's film that ends inside the tool, with the film's own frame as the open file | the reveal comes with no setup |
| **Hold the result back** | show the making before the made thing | words typed before the weather they become | the wait is longer than the viewer's patience |
| **The silence before the name** | the sound stops just before the title | Stet's one silence, on its name | silence scattered until it means nothing |
| **The gift** | a detail nobody asked for, left undeclared, that rewards looking and never stands in the way of understanding | guests whose faces are old painted portraits | a flourish that slows the argument |

Four tests for anything on screen. Does its motion show what caused the change? Take it away: what became harder to
read? Is the time it gets in proportion to how much of it is new? Could a stranger say what it means? A motif or a
colour that a cold viewer cannot name may as well not be there, and the fault-finder's telling-back (EYES.md) is
where you find out.

## The shape, and where films drop

- Give the whole film one shape: a climb to one peak, then let it ring. Rest before an arrival, and go quiet on the name.
- Introduce parts one at a time, then show them together. If the subject is a process, its steps are the order.
- Films drop at the proof: real interface or a list, small, for too short a time. Show one number or one line, large.
  A screen full of small text says only "there is a product".
- Few words: 35 to 80 on screen for 20 to 60 seconds with no voice. The picture acts the sentence.
